# Nest Ambassadors — Referral Programme Research & Execution Map

**BirdNest · Internal · Confidential**
*From an internal employee referral programme (V1) to a BirdNest partner network (the vision)*
Prepared for the Commercial Director · July 2026

---

## Executive Summary

BirdNest's Chief Commercial Officer has set a long-term vision: a partner-driven referral engine similar to **Nawy Partners** and **Carofi Partners**, where an external network of individuals and companies refers customers to BirdNest and earns commission on converted deals.

Rather than building that platform immediately, the agreed strategy is to **start internally**: launch an employee referral programme (working name: **Nest Ambassadors**) as **Version 1**, prove the referral economics with zero technology investment, and use the operational learnings to design the external partner product.

Nest Ambassadors turns every BirdNest employee into a commissioned referrer across two revenue tracks: **Sahel reservations (Track A)** and **furnishing packages (Track B)**. The programme is deliberately low-tech — a Google Form, a tracking Google Sheet, WhatsApp broadcasts, and unique referral codes — which allows a full launch within **seven days** of management sign-off. A four-week assessment gate then decides whether to scale, extend, or sunset the programme.

> **The core logic:** Prove that trusted-person referrals convert profitably for BirdNest before spending a single engineering hour. V1 is simultaneously a revenue play for the short Sahel season and a live market test that de-risks the partner-platform vision.

Three management decisions are required to go live: **(1)** commission amounts for both tracks, **(2)** the CX-team inclusion mechanism, and **(3)** the launch date. Everything else — mechanics, tracking, enablement, internal marketing, and success metrics — is defined and ready to execute.

## 1. Strategic Context: Vision vs. Version 1

### 1.1 The vision

The end state is a **BirdNest Partners** ecosystem: brokers, freelancers, property owners, and eventually companies who refer bookings and furnishing clients to BirdNest through a dedicated platform, track their pipeline, and get paid commission automatically. This is the model that **Nawy** built for real-estate brokerage and **Carofi** built for car financing — both in the Egyptian market, both proving that partner networks can become a primary acquisition channel.

### 1.2 Why we do not build the vision first

- **Unknown unit economics.** We do not yet know BirdNest's referral conversion rate, cost per booking versus paid media, or the commission level that actually motivates referrers. Building a platform on guessed numbers is expensive risk.
- **Unproven operations.** Referral validation, attribution disputes, commission payout timing, and lead handoff to Sales are operational muscles BirdNest has not exercised yet. V1 builds them manually first, so the eventual product automates a proven process instead of a hypothesis.
- **Season pressure.** The Sahel window is roughly 90 days (June–August). A platform build would miss the season entirely; a form-and-sheet programme captures it.
- **Trust and control.** Employees are a known, controllable, coachable referrer base. Fraud risk, brand risk, and lead quality are all easier to manage internally while the rules are being stress-tested.

### 1.3 The staged path

| Stage | Who refers | Infrastructure | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 — Now** | BirdNest employees (Nest Ambassadors) | Google Form + Sheet + WhatsApp + referral codes | Prove conversion & economics during Sahel season |
| **V2 — Post-season** | Trusted external circle: owners, past guests, freelancers | Referral links, light dashboard (can live on the new site) | Test external referrer behaviour with light tooling |
| **V3 — The vision** | Open partner network: brokers, agencies, ambassadors | BirdNest Partners portal/app: leads, tracking, payouts, leaderboard | Partner network as a core acquisition channel |

This is the direct translation of what was discussed in the CCO meeting: agree the vision, then **break it down to a goal we can hit in a week** — V1 — and let the operational reality of running it tell us how to build V2 and V3 correctly, instead of guessing upfront.

## 2. Benchmark Analysis

### 2.1 Nawy Partners (real estate, Egypt)

Nawy Partners is the reference model for the vision. It is a broker-enablement platform sitting on top of Nawy's proptech marketplace, reported to have grown to a network of **over 6,500 partner companies**, making it one of Egypt's largest broker-support ecosystems.

- **Free, low-friction onboarding.** Joining costs nothing; partners sign up via a form and get an account manager. Low entry friction is what let the network scale.
- **Platform value beyond commission.** A web and app dashboard gives partners inventory access (primary and resale), pipeline tracking, and direct contact with developer sales teams — the platform is a workflow tool, not just a payout ledger.
- **Money mechanics as the hook.** Partners earn the same commission rate as Nawy's own sales team, or can trade rate for speed (express commission paid in 15–30 days). Cash-flow speed is used as a retention lever.
- **Gamification and recognition.** Regular contests (cash, gold, even cars) and an annual recognition event keep the network engaged — the same psychology as our Gold/Silver Ambassador and streak bonuses, at larger scale.
- **An ambassador track already exists inside it.** Nawy's partner app includes a lighter "ambassador" mode: individuals refer interested buyers and earn commission on successful sales — effectively Nawy's version of what BirdNest is launching internally as V1.

### 2.2 Carofi Partners (auto finance, Egypt)

Carofi, an Egyptian car financing and marketplace startup, runs **Carofi Partner** — an app positioned as a professional hub for financing partners, car dealers, and freelance sellers. Partners use it to manage deals, follow up with clients, and expand reach. The takeaway for BirdNest: the partner model generalises beyond real estate, and even an early-stage company can run one — but Carofi, like Nawy, productised the partner experience **only after** the underlying commission-per-deal mechanics existed.

### 2.3 What the benchmarks tell us

- **Sequence is consistent.** Both companies layered platforms on top of proven manual commission economics. BirdNest's V1-first approach copies the sequence, not just the end state.
- **Clear rules are non-negotiable.** Both operate on explicit rules for attribution and payout. Our key conditions (new-customer-only, tracked-before-contact, Commercial Director as final arbiter) are the V1 seed of that rulebook.
- **Speed and transparency of payout drive engagement.** Nawy's express-commission option shows payout timing is a lever. BirdNest's post-season payout for Sahel is a known engagement risk to watch (see Section 5).
- **Recognition scales motivation cheaply.** Leaderboards, titles, and public celebration cost little and are proven in-market.

## 3. Programme Anatomy: How Nest Ambassadors Works

### 3.1 The model at a glance

All BirdNest employees (with CX handled as a special case — see Section 4) become Ambassadors. They refer new customers on two tracks, share a unique referral code or form link, and earn cash commission on confirmed conversions, with bonus incentives for top performers.

| Element | Track A — Sahel Reservations ("Book a Nest") | Track B — Furnishing Packages ("Furnish a Home") |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligible lead** | New customer only — never booked with BirdNest | New client only — not in the existing Sales pipeline or CRM |
| **Commission trigger** | Checkout confirmed + full payment + no cancellation/refund | Deal closed + full payment received by Sales |
| **Payment timing** | Post-season (September 2026) | Upon deal-closure confirmation |
| **Amount** | To be set by management | To be set by management |
| **Exclusions** | Employee discounts / special rates do not apply | Employee discounts / special rates do not apply |

### 3.2 The referral journey (five steps)

1. **Identify.** The Ambassador spots a friend, family member, or contact looking to book in Sahel or furnish a home.
2. **Submit.** They fill the Referral Submission Form (Google Form / WhatsApp link) with contact details and product track.
3. **Validate.** Commercial ops checks the contact against the CRM and confirms they are new to BirdNest.
4. **Follow up.** Furnishing leads hand off to Sales; Sahel customers book through the normal channel using the referral code.
5. **Confirm.** Booking checkout confirmed or furnishing deal closed and paid — commission is triggered and logged.

### 3.3 Tracking stack — no tech build required

- **Google Form:** referral submission (Ambassador name, department, lead contact, track, timestamp).
- **Google Sheet:** auto-populated log; commercial team adds validation status, conversion status, and commission due.
- **WhatsApp broadcast group:** programme updates, leaderboard standings, conversion confirmations.
- **Referral codes:** one unique code per employee (e.g. `NEST-[initials]-01`), quoted by referred customers at booking.

### 3.4 Incentive architecture

| Incentive | Criteria | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **Base commission** | Confirmed conversion on either track | Cash — amount set by management |
| **Gold Ambassador** | Most confirmed deals/bookings in the period | Premium cash bonus + "Ambassador of the Season" recognition + featured in company comms |
| **Silver Ambassador** | 2nd highest confirmed conversions | Cash bonus + company-wide shout-out |
| **First Blood Bonus** | First Ambassador to land both a confirmed booking AND a furnishing deal | Surprise bonus announced at launch to drive early action |
| **Streak Bonus** | 3+ confirmed referrals across both tracks | Cash multiplier on 3rd+ referral commission |

### 3.5 Integrity rules (key conditions)

- New customers only — the referral must not already exist in BirdNest's customer database.
- No employee discounts, special rates, or personal benefits apply to referred bookings or deals.
- The referral must be logged through the programme form **before** the customer contacts BirdNest through any other channel.
- If a customer already came in via marketing or another channel, the referral claim is invalid.
- Disputes are resolved by the Commercial Director; the decision is final.

## 4. The CX Question

The CX team receives all inbound leads from marketing and sales channels. Without robust CRM attribution there is no reliable way to tell a lead a CX agent genuinely referred from their personal network apart from an inbound lead they simply handled. Left unmanaged, CX agents could claim commission on any inbound lead, making the programme economically unviable and creating resentment across departments — which is why CX is excluded by default, with two proposed inclusion mechanisms:

| Option | Mechanism | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Recommended** | Pre-submission before contact: the CX agent's form timestamp must predate any CRM record of the contact; if a lead appears in CRM first, the claim is invalid. | Clean, enforceable, timestamp-based, zero tech. Recommended for V1. |
| **B — Alternative** | Dedicated CX track: only contacts with zero prior BirdNest touchpoints; agent submits the customer's name plus a personal note; Sales manager verifies novelty in CRM. | Clear audit trail, but adds manual verification workload. |

**Recommendation:** adopt Option A for V1 (or confirm CX exclusion for Phase 1 and revisit at the Week-4 gate). The timestamp rule also generalises cleanly to external partners later — it is the same attribution logic a V3 platform would automate.

## 5. Core Reasoning: Why V1 Is the Right Move Now

- **The season is short and live.** The Sahel peak is a ~90-day window; every incremental June–August booking matters to GMV and occupancy. A 7-day launch captures the remaining season; anything slower forfeits it.
- **Referrals out-convert ads.** A recommendation from a known person converts far more reliably than a paid ad impression — the entire premise of both benchmark programmes.
- **The furnishing pipeline needs top-of-funnel.** Sales capacity exists; what's missing is trusted-referrer lead volume. The programme feeds exactly that gap.
- **Zero tech dependency.** A WhatsApp form and a Google Sheet is the whole stack — live in days, not months, and disposable if the Week-4 gate says kill.
- **It is a paid experiment that pays for itself.** Commission is only paid on confirmed, fully-paid conversions, so the downside is bounded; a failed programme costs almost nothing but the team's attention.
- **Every output is an input to the vision.** Conversion rates, cost per booking, dispute frequency, and validation workload are precisely the numbers needed to write the business case and product requirements for the partner platform.

### 5.1 What V1 must teach us (the learning agenda)

| Question | V1 signal | Feeds into the vision as… |
|---|---|---|
| Do referred leads convert, and at what rate? | Validation rate; conversion rate per track | Revenue model for the partner platform |
| What does a referred booking cost? | Commission paid ÷ confirmed bookings vs. paid-media CPB | Commission strategy and margin design |
| Which product pulls referrals? | Track A vs. Track B volume and quality mix | Which product leads partner recruitment |
| How heavy is validation & attribution? | Ops hours per referral; dispute rate | Feature list for automation (CRM checks, timestamps) |
| What motivates referrers? | Activation rate; repeat referral rate; bonus responsiveness | Incentive design for external partners |
| Where does the process break? | Handoff failures, code misuse, channel confusion | Rulebook and UX for the partner product |

This is the throughline the CCO asked for: **V1 is not a side project — it is the R&D phase of the partner platform**, run at zero build cost, on a live revenue season, with real employees as the test panel.

## 6. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Attribution disputes** | Erodes trust and can poison team culture fast | Tracked-before-contact rule; timestamps as the single source of truth; Commercial Director as final arbiter, stated upfront |
| **Post-season payout deflates momentum** | Track A commission lands in September — a long wait for a summer effort | Weekly WhatsApp confirmations of "commission locked"; visible leaderboard; First Blood and streak bonuses paid faster as morale fuel |
| **Low activation** | Below ~30% activation, the programme lacks the volume to prove anything | Leadership launch message, 45-minute orientation, ready-made toolkit (code, fact sheet, WhatsApp templates), First Blood urgency |
| **Lead quality dilution** | Employees may submit weak leads to chase bonuses | New-customer-only validation; lead-quality criteria taught in orientation; validation rate tracked weekly as a health metric |
| **CX resentment (either direction)** | Exclusion feels unfair; naive inclusion is exploitable | Transparent explanation of the attribution problem + Option A timestamp mechanism as the fairness bridge |
| **Sheet chaos at volume** | Manual tracking degrades if submissions spike | Locked column structure, single owner in commercial ops, weekly reconciliation; a good problem that triggers V2 tooling |
| **Commission set too low (or too high)** | Too low kills motivation; too high kills margin | Benchmark against paid-media CPB before setting; treat V1 amounts as testable, revisit at Week-4 gate |

## 7. Success Metrics & the Week-4 Gate

Metrics are measured weekly from launch, with an assessment gate at the end of Week 4 to decide on continuation.

| Cluster | Metric | Target / use |
|---|---|---|
| Participation | Activation rate — % of eligible employees submitting ≥1 referral in first 2 weeks | > 30% |
| Participation | Referral submission volume | Awareness & engagement indicator |
| Participation | Validation rate — % passing the CRM novelty check | Lead-quality indicator |
| Sahel bookings | Confirmed bookings from referrals (paid, checkout-confirmed) | Primary volume KPI |
| Sahel bookings | GMV from referrals | Assessed post-checkout |
| Sahel bookings | Referral cost per booking (commission ÷ bookings) | Compare vs. paid-media CPB |
| Furnishing | Leads submitted / leads in active pipeline / conversion rate | Pipeline contribution |
| Programme health | Repeat referral rate; time to first conversion; dispute rate | Sustained engagement & integrity |

**Week-4 decision: scale or kill.** If conversions justify cost, extend the programme to the full Sahel season and expand the furnishing incentive — and begin scoping V2. If not, sunset with documented learnings. Either outcome produces the data the vision needs.

## 8. Execution Map (Summary)

The full day-by-day plan, ownership matrix, and build checklist live in the companion document, [**Nest Ambassadors — Quick-Win Action Plan**](./02-quick-win-action-plan.md). In summary:

- **Phase 0 — Decisions** (management, ~1 meeting). Confirm commission amounts for both tracks, choose the CX path (Option A recommended), confirm the programme name, and greenlight Day 1.
- **Phase 1 — Days 1–2:** Design & approvals. Finalise commissions, CX mechanism, eligibility, name and branding sign-off.
- **Phase 2 — Days 3–4:** Infrastructure. Build the form, set up the tracking sheet, generate referral codes for all staff, create the WhatsApp Ambassadors group.
- **Phase 3 — Day 5:** Enablement. Company-wide 45-minute orientation by Sales (both tracks), distribute the Ambassador Toolkit, live Q&A.
- **Phase 4 — Day 6:** Internal launch. Leadership announcement email, ambassador graphics, First Blood teaser, optional 60-second CEO/Commercial Director video.
- **Phase 5 — Day 7:** Go live. Submissions open, commercial ops on standby for validations, leaderboard tracking begins, first broadcast sent.
- **Weeks 1–4:** Operate & decide. Weekly pulse checks (Weeks 1–2), impact assessment (Weeks 3–4), then the scale-or-kill gate.
